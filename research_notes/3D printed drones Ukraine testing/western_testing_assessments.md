## 1. Which Western militaries have tested printed drones, what were the results (reliability, endurance, payload, cost, failures), and what have they decided (adopt, pilot, reject)?

### Takeaway
Western militaries are running exercise-level experiments and small in-house builds (US Army, USMC, US Navy, UK Army), plus startup contracts for printed-airframe systems (Air Force/Firestorm), but none has published quantitative test data and none has declared a printed-airframe FPV a program of record. Decisions to date are "experiment / pilot / limited fielding / fund the manufacturing cell" - no formal rejection was found, though a UK-linked volunteer reports Ukrainian units abandoned printing "hundreds of times".

Airframe printable overnight, assembled without specialised tools; printed airframe is the cheap, crash-absorbing part; total cost "just over $1,000". ### Inferences
- Decision status by actor (my synthesis): US Army = experiment/pilot at unit and lab level, with ARL SPARTA the most formal (soldier-feedback loop, handed to 11th ABN); USMC = pilot of an organic, NDAA-compliant, NAVAIR-interim-cleared HANX while *buying* conventional Archers at scale (so the Corps is hedging, not adopting printing as the primary supply route); Navy = demonstration of containerised production; USAF = funding the manufacturing system (xCell/Tempest) rather than validating a specific printed FPV; UK = single-exercise proof of concept, no adoption decision.
- The recurring stated value proposition is logistics/repair/iteration speed at the point of need and low unit cost (~$700-$3,000 claims), not demonstrated superior airframe performance. No source offers a head-to-head with conventional airframes.
- Cost claims are not apples-to-apples: HANX ($700), SPARTA (~$1,000), Hohenfels ($2-3K), UK (GBP 400) probably exclude labour, printer capex, validated electronics/payload; treat as indicative.
- Weak institutional capacity to certify parts (airworthiness, supply-chain/NDAA compliance, Chinese-printer concerns) appears to be a binding constraint at least as much as printing capability.

## 2. What do named experts and institutions conclude about real-world performance of printed airframes vs conventional drones? Any dissenting or sceptical views?

### Takeaway
I found almost no think-tank (CSIS/CNAS/RUSI/RAND/WOTR/MWI) analysis that assesses printed-airframe performance directly; the usable evidence is practitioner statements, official unit accounts and vendor/hobbyist engineering commentary. The weight of evidence suggests printing is valued for repair, iteration and local supply rather than for flight performance, with named sceptical views from a volunteer (printing "tried hundreds of times" and abandoned in Ukraine) and from the Army's own reported parts-approval and early-test-failure problems.

### Cited Findings
- **Sceptical, named volunteer.** Volunteer Richard Woodruff said 3D printing had been tried "hundreds of times" and abandoned by drone units in Ukraine, so the approach is not automatically a success (cited alongside the Irish Guards story; LBC's headline frames critics saying MoD is "lagging behind Russia"). **[ANECDOTE via PRESS]** — [AOL/Business Insider](https://www.aol.com/news/ukraine-showed-british-unit-needs-124955510.html); [LBC](https://www.lbc.co.uk/news/exclusive/british-armys-first-3d-printing-drone-hub)
- **Pro-printing Ukrainian practitioner (anonymous).** A Wild Hornets spokesperson (anonymous, via DOU/TechRadar) defends printers as critical, says the unit uses desktop FDM printers (Bambu Lab, Elegoo) because they lower cost and speed scaling versus industrial systems, and calls drone "swarms" largely a myth; AI adds an estimated $150-$500 per drone. Production-rate claims conflict (about 100/day per TechRadar vs about 1,000/day per 3D Printing Industry). Wild Hornets also makes the Sting interceptor. **[ANECDOTE/COMPANY via PRESS]** — [TechRadar](https://www.techradar.com/pro/anonymous-wild-hornets-spokesperson-calls-drone-swarms-a-fun-legend-and-a-scam-mechanism-as-he-defends-the-critical-importance-of-3d-printers-in-warfare); [3D Printing Industry](https://3dprintingindustry.com/news/ukraine-deploys-3d-printed-drones-to-combat-russian-shahed-swarms-242362/)
- **US Army practitioners.** Hohenfels team: early tests failed and frames were redesigned; parts approval is the hardest part. - **ARL framing.** In SPARTA the printed airframe is explicitly treated as the cheap, replaceable, crash-absorbing element; the lab's 2017 feedback list (noise, standoff, payload, agility) shows earlier printed UAS were deficient there. **[OFFICIAL]** — [Army.mil 2026](https://www.army.mil/article/291172); [Army.mil 2017](https://www.army.mil/article/180189/army_researchers_demonstrate_3_d_printed_drones)

- **Engineering trade-offs (low-authority sources).** Hobbyist/vendor blogs: printed FPV frames typically 2-3x heavier than carbon-fibre for equal stiffness (e.g. ~120-180 g vs ~60-80 g for a 5-inch frame), arms flex at high G, layer-line micro-cracks and fatigue can cause sudden failure, common plastics soften with electronics heat, vibration transmission can overwhelm gyro filtering; CF-filled nylons narrow the gap; hybrid carbon arms + printed centre is the common compromise; printed frames judged viable for small (<=2.5 in) builds. Filament for a frame is roughly $5-$15 while a 5-inch FPV build costs ~$150-250, i.e. electronics/motors/batteries dominate cost. **[VENDOR-BLOG]** — [UAVMODEL](https://blog.uavmodel.com/3d-printed-fpv-drone-frames-performance-durability-and-reality-check-2026/); [FPVDroneFrame](https://fpvdroneframe.com/cnc-drone-frames-vs-3d-printed-drone-frames/); [Show HN thread on printed VTOL (battery mass fraction)](https://news.ycombinator.com/item?id=44241278)
- **Vendor/market claims (flag as marketing).** AM Research: drone-related printing ~$140M in 2025 rising toward ~$900M by 2034; HP says one printer can support >7,000 small quadcopters/month; BigRep says ~$10,000 conventional vs ~$2,500 printed per airframe segment; another summary cites a $3.3B FY2026 AM budget line, which I could not verify. **[VENDOR/MARKET]** — [3DPrint.com](https://3dprint.com/328406/uas-additive-strategies-shows-how-fast-drone-manufacturing-is-changing/); [DroneLife/HP](https://dronelife.com/2026/02/27/additive-manufacturing-drone-production-hp/); [DroneLife, XPONENTIAL 2026](https://dronelife.com/2026/05/21/us-drone-makers-are-building-a-different-kind-of-factory/); [BigRep](https://bigrep.com/posts/role-of-3d-printing-in-modern-warfare/); [3D Printing Industry](https://3dprintingindustry.com/news/why-drone-economics-are-driving-growth-in-3d-printed-components-253790/)
- **Security/supply-chain concern.** UK use of China-made Bambu Lab printers drew security criticism; HANX was explicitly marketed as NDAA-compliant, noting earlier Marine printed drones were not subject to the same NDAA requirements. **[PRESS]** — [Defense Post](https://thedefensepost.com/2025/11/19/uk-3d-printers-china); [3DPrinting.com](https://3dprinting.com/news/marine-corps-develops-first-ndaa-compliant-3d-printed-drone)

#### Other conflicts (high-level cross-check; reliability flagged)
- **Myanmar (best-documented non-Ukraine case).** Resistance (PDF) groups produce printed frames/parts and munition components; named printed drones cited include Liberator MK1 (Dec 2022) and MK2. Reported scale: Operation 1027 (late 2023) >25,000 bombs dropped by resistance groups (Dhaka Tribune puts it near 20,000). Jamestown argues printing lets insurgents field drones without conventional supply chains; Dhaka Tribune reports resistance groups losing their drone edge partly because China restricted dual-use exports. Reliability: **[PRESS/think-tank]**, moderate; mostly unverified counts; no performance data. — [Jamestown](https://jamestown.org/anti-junta-rebels-resort-to-3d-printed-weapons-in-myanmar/); [The Diplomat](https://magazine.thediplomat.com/2025-04/the-impact-of-technological-weapons-in-the-myanmar-conflict); [Dhaka Tribune](https://www.dhakatribune.com/world/asia/386185/myanmar-s-armed-rebel-groups-lose-edge-in-drone); [Bellingcat, 2022](https://www.bellingcat.com/news/2022/01/20/myanmars-rebels-get-resourceful-with-improvised-drones); [Security-Risks](https://security-risks.com/post/myanmar-pdf-producing-3-d-weapons-drones)
- **Syria/Middle East non-state.** Analyst Michael Horton said the Houthis have already printed some drone parts (thin, second-hand); HTS reportedly makes similar drones locally; al-Shabaab was among the first jihadist groups to experiment with printing; UK case of an IS-linked plot to build a printed drone in Coventry (2023). **[PRESS/ANECDOTE]**, low-moderate. — [The National, Dec 2024](https://www.thenationalnews.com/news/mena/2024/12/04/syria-militants-drones-war); [Lawfare](https://www.lawfaremedia.org/article/hayat-tahrir-al-sham-s-drone-force); [Jamestown](https://jamestown.org/interest-in-3d-printed-firearms-rising-among-islamist-militants/); [CoFutures](https://www.cofutures.net/post/islamic-state-3d-printed-weaponized-drone-plot-in-coventry-uk)
- **Israel/Hamas/Iran.** Israel's IAI/MoD showed a 26-part printed UAV in 2015 (Janes); a June 2025 Fabbaloo report alleges Israeli agents used 3D printing inside Iran for covert drone manufacture - unverified, low reliability; for Hamas, sources point to Iranian know-how/Chinese-style components, not printing. **[PRESS; low]** — [Janes](https://janes.com/defence-news/israels-first-3d-printed-uav-takes-to-the-skies); [Fabbaloo](https://www.fabbaloo.com/news/israel-allegedly-used-3d-printing-inside-iran-for-covert-drone-manufacturing); [TWZ](https://www.twz.com/40601/palestinian-militants-are-now-launching-suicide-drones-at-israel.md)
- **Sudan (RSF/SAF) and Mexican cartels.** No reporting found linking either to printed drones; RSF drones appear imported (Chinese-made and others per Yale/Al Jazeera/New Arab coverage). **[PRESS]** — [Al Jazeera, Feb 2026](https://www.aljazeera.com/news/longform/2026/2/3/drone-warfare-in-sudan-tracking-1000-aerial-attacks-since-april-2023); [NPR, Mar 2026](https://www.npr.org/2026/03/18/nx-s1-5752387/how-drones-are-being-used-globally-in-conflicts-and-by-criminals)

### Inferences
- The consistent signal across US/UK practitioner accounts and engineering commentary is that printing optimises cost-of-repair and iteration, and that airframe mass/stiffness/fatigue penalties push printed designs toward short-range, expendable or training/recon roles. This is inference, not a measured result.
- Because no think-tank assessment was found, claims by Firestorm or the vendors that printed airframes match conventional performance remain untested publicly.
- The sceptical Ukraine-linked view (Woodruff) should be weighted as a single anecdote; it conflicts with the Wild Hornets claim of heavy printer reliance, suggesting Ukrainian practice is split (printing for components/interceptors and repairs vs fully conventional airframes) - needs resolving against the Ukraine-side notes.
- Non-Ukraine conflicts offer weak cross-check value: Myanmar is the only well-reported case and it shows printing as a substitute for blocked supply chains (and the limit - the edge eroded when electronics/dual-use imports were cut), consistent with printing not being the bottleneck.

### Gaps
- No CSIS, CNAS, RUSI, RAND, War on the Rocks or Modern War Institute piece on printed-airframe performance was located (search tool returned vendors and hobbyists); a RUSI search returned only an unrelated air-defence report. Direct site searches are needed.
- No named Western general/official quoted assessing printed vs conventional performance beyond unit-level statements.
- Myanmar figures (bomb counts, drone numbers) are not independently verified; no data on Houthi/Hezbollah/HTS printed-airframe usage beyond single mentions.

## 3. Which exercises/test events produced public findings on printed drones?

### Takeaway
Public findings are thin and come mostly from service public-affairs articles and press relays of unit statements: UK Exercise Bull Storm 2025, US Army events (Hohenfels/Poland 2025, Lethal Eagle Jan 2025, Agile Spirit 25, Best Drone Warfighter Competition Mar 2026), TREX 25-2, RIMPAC 2026 (Essex/xCell) and Grafenwoehr Sept 2026. NATO TIE counter-drone events and Brave1's Test in Ukraine programme produced no printed-drone-specific public findings that I could find.

### Cited Findings
- **Exercise Bull Storm, Kenya (May 2025):** five printed FPVs; ~3.5 h print + ~1 h assembly each; outcome of mock battle unaffected; follow-up ambition for Bull Storm 26. **[OFFICIAL]** — [Soldier Magazine](https://soldier.army.mod.uk/issues/july-2025/updates/3d-printed-drones)
- **JMRC Hohenfels / Poland exercise (Apr 2025):** printed sensor drones; early test failures, redesign, parts-approval delays; no published exercise result. **[PRESS]** — [Stars and Stripes](https://www.stripes.com/branches/army/2025-04-18/army-unit-in-germany-builds-their-own-drones-17505745.html); [Defense Post](https://thedefensepost.com/2025/04/30/us-army-3d-drones-2)
- **Operation Lethal Eagle (101st ABN, Jan 2025, 21 days):** ~100 printed sUAS; qualitative positive feedback only. **[OFFICIAL]** — [Army.mil](https://www.army.mil/article/282644/101st_airborne_division_takes_flight_with_3d_printed_drones)
- **Agile Spirit 25:** 173rd deployed printers to build FPVs in the field (no findings published). **[PRESS]** — [3Dnatives](https://www.3dnatives.com/en/u-s-soldiers-are-using-3d-printing-to-build-battlefield-ready-drones-06082025/)
- **Technology & Readiness Experimentation (TREX) 25-2 (Aug 2025):** in-flight printing of drone parts on a UH-60. **[OFFICIAL via trade]** — [Mobility Engineering Tech](https://www.mobilityengineeringtech.com/component/content/article/54049-in-flight-3d-printing-of-drone-parts-on-a-black-hawk-helicopter)

- **RIMPAC 2026 / USS Essex (Jul-Aug 2026):** 12 Squall FPVs printed at sea; flown as adversary aircraft in a counter-drone event. Company-sourced; no official findings. **[COMPANY via PRESS]** — [DroneLife](https://dronelife.com/2026/08/27/firestorm-labs-drone-manufacturing-at-sea/)

- **NATO Technology Interoperability Exercises (TIE):** TIE23 (Sep 2023) ~70 counter-drone systems; TIE24 (Sep 2024) Ukraine first participation, >60 commercial C-UAS systems; TIE26 (May 2026, Netherlands) ~300 participants including interceptor concepts, with successful systems eligible for Baltic Trust 26 (Latvia, Aug 2026). All focus on counter-drone interoperability, with no printed-drone findings (threat drones used as targets were not described as printed). **[OFFICIAL]** — [NATO](https://www.nato.int/cps/en/natohq/news_218655.htm); [NCIA](https://www.ncia.nato.int/newsroom/news/allies-and-industry-test-the-latest-counterdrone-technology-during-nato-exercise)

- **Project Convergence:** searches did not surface any printed-drone findings (not specifically searched beyond general queries). **No source found.**

### Inferences
- The "findings" from these events are qualitative and promotional in character; none provides pass/fail criteria, sample sizes or statistics. The most credible public artefacts are the Army.mil/DVIDS items on SPARTA and the 101st and the Soldier magazine piece, because they are first-party (though still PR).
- The Hohenfels admission of failed early tests and the Bull Storm "no effect on outcome" statement are the more candid data points; most other accounts report only successes.

### Gaps
- No Project Convergence, NATO hackathon/DIANA, Baltic Trust 26, or Ukraine-Western joint-trial finding on printed drones located.
- JMRC/JRTC after-action reports, Poland exercise results, Bull Storm 26 (2026) results, and any official Navy RIMPAC assessment were not found.
- No evidence of Western-participant reports from Brave1 test ranges naming printed airframes.

## 4. Which defense companies/startups sell expeditionary printed drones, and what independent validation exists for their claims?

Firestorm Labs (San Diego) is the clearest US vendor of expeditionary printed-drone production (xCell + Tempest/Squall), backed by real but ceiling-based government contracts; independent validation of its performance claims is essentially absent beyond contract awards and a company-sourced RIMPAC demo.
